## 1. Executive summary

Public references this week were limited but meaningful. The clearest in-window item was an EEEI archive update on 2026-07-07 amplifying the CEPEJ 46th plenary discussion on digital transformation, automation and human-centred justice. LinkedIn-accessible posts from CCBE Secretary-General Simone Cuomo and the Council of Europe Office in Chisinau continued to circulate CEPEJ's AI guidance, especially the Guidelines on the Use of Generative AI for Courts and the 2018 European Ethical Charter.

The broader source set shows a consistent pattern: CEPEJ's AI work is being cited less as a standalone ethical charter and more as an operational governance toolkit for courts. Current public references connect the 2018 Charter, the 2023 Assessment Tool, the AI Advisory Board, the Resource Centre on Cyberjustice and AI, the 2025 generative AI Guidelines, and plans for an Observatory on AI in Justice.

## 7. Trends and interpretation

1. From principles to operational controls. Public references increasingly treat CEPEJ's 2018 Ethical Charter as the foundation for concrete tools: the Assessment Tool, AIAB reflections, Resource Centre, generative AI Guidelines and a proposed Observatory on AI in Justice.

2. Human-centred justice is the dominant frame. Across EEEI, UIHJ, LinkedIn and CEPEJ sources, the recurring line is that AI may support efficiency but must not replace judicial responsibility, reasoning or accountability.

3. Generative AI has become the practical pressure point. The 2025 CEPEJ Guidelines are repeatedly cited because courts are already using or considering standard tools such as general-purpose assistants, translation tools and summarisation systems.

4. Data sovereignty and institutional control are rising themes. CEPEJ-GT-CYBERJUST references now connect AI governance with data sovereignty, open-source considerations, public authority control over infrastructure and traceability.

5. Training is moving into the foreground. EuroDIG, HELP, EJTN, UNESCO and CEPEJ references point to judicial AI literacy as a core implementation need, not a secondary dissemination activity.

6. Professional stakeholders are amplifying CEPEJ. CCBE, UIHJ and EEEI references show that CEPEJ's AI outputs are being carried by bar, enforcement, expert and judicial networks rather than only by Council of Europe channels.

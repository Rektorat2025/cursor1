## 7. Trends and interpretation

1. **Post-plenary lull.** After June’s 46th plenary and mid-July’s EEEI/Rule of Law signals, this week produced no new date-verified public references. That is consistent with a quiet midsummer interval rather than a reversal of interest in CEPEJ AI work.
2. **Date hygiene remains essential.** Search results again mis-surfaced older CEPEJ-related pages as if current (notably BIICL “AI in the Courts” material that resolves to a **9 June 2025** event, not a 2026 date). HTTP `Last-Modified` headers on LGSEE, Juristech, and Uniandes pages moved during this window without matching content `dateModified` / `datePublished` changes—so headers alone are insufficient to count a weekly mention.
3. **Human-centred framing is stable.** Carry-over commentary continues to treat CEPEJ instruments as the operational reference for non-substitution, oversight, transparency, and data sovereignty—especially when national rules (Spain’s Instruction 2/2026 / CTEAJE Policy) are mapped onto the Charter.
4. **Informal adoption remains the live governance problem.** The Kunda “silent AI use” thesis and the EEEI survey awareness gap remain the most actionable recent themes: CEPEJ guidance is being invoked less as abstract ethics and more as a response to bottom-up professional use.
5. **Watchpoints for the next cycles.** Per EEEI’s WG-CYBERJUST reporting: next Cyberjust meeting **22–23 October 2026**; 6th European Cyberjustice Network meeting **2 December 2026**. Broader EU context: high-risk AI Act obligations approaching full applicability in August 2026 may increase secondary citations of CEPEJ tools even when CEPEJ itself is quiet.

## 1. Executive summary

- The main public CEPEJ-AI reference found during the reporting week was the **EuroDIG 2026 session "Legal Professionals Navigating E-learning and AI"** on **26 May 2026**, initiated by **CEPEJ** and the Council of Europe's **HELP** programme. It explicitly pointed readers to the CEPEJ Resource Centre on Cyberjustice and AI, the 2025 generative-AI guidelines for courts, the 2024 generative-AI information note, the 2018 European Ethical Charter, and the 2023 Assessment Tool.
- Media coverage of EuroDIG amplified the same message: CEPEJ's AI work is increasingly framed as a **training, governance, and rule-of-law issue**, not only as court technology.

## 7. Trends and interpretation

- **Training is becoming the public-facing theme.** The week's strongest CEPEJ mention was not a new standard but a public session on training legal professionals for AI. This aligns with the CEPEJ 2026-2027 roadmap's planned ICT training curriculum for judges.
- **CEPEJ's AI corpus is now layered.** Public references increasingly treat CEPEJ's approach as a sequence: 2018 Ethical Charter -> 2023 Assessment Tool -> 2024 generative-AI note -> 2025 generative-AI court guidelines -> 2026-2027 Observatory/AIAB roadmap.
- **Generative AI risks have moved from abstraction to court operations.** Brazilian coverage of prompt injection and hallucinated citations did not directly cite CEPEJ, but it validates the same risk categories: human oversight, traceability, data security, and prohibition on delegating judicial reasoning to machines.
- **The CEPEJ Resource Centre/Observatory is gaining strategic visibility.** EEEI and CEPEJ-related sources repeatedly describe the planned transformation of the Resource Centre into an Observatory on AI in Justice to support the Council of Europe Framework Convention on AI in the judicial field.
- **AI Act discourse is increasing pressure on judicial AI governance.** Legal commentary on high-risk AI systems and judicial/legal use cases continues to make CEPEJ-style controls more relevant, even where CEPEJ is not directly cited.
- **Terminology caveat:** Many public references use "CEPEJ AI Charter" informally, but the official title remains **European Ethical Charter on the use of artificial intelligence in judicial systems and their environment**.

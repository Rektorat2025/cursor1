**Scope:** Public references to the CEPEJ AI Charter; CEPEJ and artificial intelligence; the European Commission for the Efficiency of Justice and AI; ethical AI in justice; and AI in judicial systems.

## 1. Executive summary

- This window produced **one newly public text** that discusses CEPEJ work on AI. It is an observer account, not a new CEPEJ instrument. The **International Union of Judicial Officers (UIHJ)** published, under the byline **PG-uihj** and the visible date **28 October 2026**, a report of the **second meeting of CEPEJ-GT-EFF** in Strasbourg on **27–28 October 2026**. The note says the recast of the 2009 enforcement guidelines now covers the use of artificial intelligence tools by enforcement agents, and that the working group agreed to send the current draft to the CEPEJ for adoption at the **47th plenary on 3–4 December 2026**. The same text exists in French at a parallel URL. It is one account in two languages. It names no Charter principle, no paragraph 58, no paragraph 61, and no CEPEJ document number for the draft.
- The **CEPEJ newsroom** published its own account of that same meeting. Google News dates the item **28 October 2026, 19:02:12 UTC**, source `coe.int`, byline in the search index **Lucas Mathieu**. The indexed paragraphs describe a draft revision of the 2009 enforcement guidelines and do not contain the words artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, or Charter. Direct HTML for `coe.int` returned **HTTP 403**, so the newsroom date was not read from a schema tag on the page.
- Two further CEPEJ newsroom items are now visible in the search index and were not readable on 26 October, when direct `coe.int` requests also returned HTTP 403. Google News dates them **before** this window: the secretariat note on the **15th CEPEJ-GT-CYBERJUST** meeting (deepfakes, automation, data sovereignty, the AI Observatory) at **22 October 2026, 07:00:00 UTC**, and the note on the **41st CEPEJ-GT-QUAL** meeting at **20 October 2026, 07:00:00 UTC**. ## 7. Trends and interpretation

1. **The week’s CEPEJ AI news is an enforcement draft moving toward adoption.** On 25 October the public record said GT-EFF had asked GT-CYBERJUST to review AI and cybersecurity sections of draft enforcement guidelines. On 28 October the UIHJ record says those subjects are in the recast, including “use of artificial intelligence tools by enforcement agents”, and that GT-EFF agreed to submit the current draft to the 47th plenary on 3–4 December 2026. The draft text is not public. Whether the AI section survives into the text that is actually tabled is the next concrete question.
2. **The secretariat note and the observer note do not carry the same facts.** The CEPEJ newsroom item of 28 October, 19:02:12 UTC, tells a reader that revised enforcement guidelines were examined. It does not mention artificial intelligence. The UIHJ note of the same meeting does. For monitoring, the observer note is the one that documents the AI content.
3. **The missing secretariat account of the 22–23 October cyberjustice meeting is now visible, and it is dated in the previous window.** Google News places it on 22 October. It confirms deepfakes and electronic evidence, automation with and without AI, data sovereignty, the Observatory and Resource Centre, the 2 December Cyberjustice Network meeting on automated justice, and coordination with GT-EVAL and GT-EFF. It is less specific than the UIHJ note of 25 October: no named rapporteurs. It still cites no article of the Charter and no paragraph of the generative-AI guidelines.
4. **The GT-QUAL write-up is also now visible, dated 20 October, and its “Charter” is a different instrument.** The indexed text is about court users, surveys, environment, expert guidelines, and quality indicators. The word Charter there means a people-centred justice charter. It should not be filed as a new citation of the 2018 Ethical Charter. The October note also does not say that the expert-guideline revision covers AI, although the April meeting report had set that as an aim.
5. **Paragraph 58 and paragraph 61 were not picked up.** Sapuppo’s English `modified_gmt` is still 13 October. The Jev post is still 13 October. Bernaziuk published again on 28 October and used “CEPEJ” only to deny institutional authority. He did not return to paragraph 61. No text this week sets his 2 October reading beside Peralta’s September dissent. Peralta is not named in the 28 October enforcement note.
6. **The 2018 Charter had no new public citation in this window.** Last week’s Brazilian commentaries (JOTA on 20 October, Zenodo on 25 October) remain the latest located uses of the Charter as a benchmark. Nothing in this window pairs the Charter with CEPEJ(2025)18Final.

Malachevsky’s 12 October statement of the 2 December 2027 date remains the latest located correction.

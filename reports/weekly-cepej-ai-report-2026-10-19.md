The Charter lists five principles and describes user control as precluding a prescriptive approach and keeping users informed and in control of their choices. She does not quote the December 2025 sentence that judges need not justify departure.

## 7. Trends and interpretation

1. **The week’s CEPEJ-AI references are comparative and doctrinal, not commentary on a fresh court incident.** Malachevsky sorts national and European instruments. Tabacu tests Article 6 against autonomous hearing. Gonzalo turns the five principles into a teaching sequence. The Lima Norte annulment, which is the week’s court story about fabricated citations, does not name CEPEJ on the page that was read. The pattern of 7–8 October, when the Santiago prompt produced Charter commentary, did not repeat for Peru.
2. **CEPEJ(2025)18Final gained a third public reading, still without paragraph numbers.** Malachevsky is the new one. Stepanova (5 October) and Bernaziuk (2 October) remain the earlier ones. Malachevsky’s contribution is structural: the guidelines are non-binding, they are a different legal object from the AI Act, and they exemplify an “international rights-based” architecture. He also restates, with the 12 October date on the page, that the Annex III high-risk timetable runs to 2 December 2027.

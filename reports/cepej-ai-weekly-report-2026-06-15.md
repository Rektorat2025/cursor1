## 7. Trends and interpretation

1. **From principles to operations.** Public references increasingly present CEPEJ's AI work as an implementation stack: 2018 Ethical Charter -> 2023/2025 Assessment Tool -> 2024 generative AI note -> 2025 AIAB report/reflections -> 2025 GenAI court guidelines -> 2026-2027 Observatory/ICT curriculum work.

2. **Human judicial control is the central repeated theme.** Across official CEPEJ documents, LinkedIn posts, EU legal materials, and national presentations, the most repeated message is that AI may assist but must not replace judges, judicial reasoning, or accountability.

3. **Generative AI has shifted CEPEJ visibility.** The CEPEJ Charter is still cited as the foundation, but public attention has moved toward generative AI use in courts, hallucinations, draft decisions, data sovereignty, and secure/customised tools.

4. **The next CEPEJ AI milestone is institutional, not just textual.** The EEEI and CEPEJ activity programme references point toward the Observatory on AI in Justice and ICT training curriculum for judges. This suggests CEPEJ's AI work is moving from guidance documents to monitoring, capacity-building, and recurring reporting.

5. **Legal-policy uptake remains durable.** EUR-Lex references from 2021/2022 show that CEPEJ's Charter has already been incorporated into EU-level parliamentary reasoning on AI in justice. Newer public commentary continues to rely on the same Charter plus the Assessment Tool and GenAI Guidelines.

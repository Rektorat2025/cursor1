#### 2.A Valentina Grazia Sapuppo — reservation of human adjudication and CEPEJ paragraph 58 (23 September 2026)

- **Date:** WordPress `date` **2026-09-23T19:43:36** (site offset); `date_gmt` / `modified_gmt` **2026-09-23T17:43:36Z**. Visible dateline “Sep 23, 2026”. English and Italian records share the timestamp.
- **Author:** Closing line “Author: Valentina Grazia Sapuppo”. WordPress author display name is the site, “Law & Technology”. A 3 July 2026 NicFab profile of the same project identifies her as the lawyer behind it (that profile does not name CEPEJ; see §4).
- **Type:** Practitioner essay on a specialist law-and-technology site, published in English and Italian at the same time.
- **URLs:**
  - English: [The law is not an algorithm…](https://lawandtechnology.eu/en/law-not-algorithm-reservation-human-adjudication-ai-act-cepej/)
  - Italian: [Il diritto non è un algoritmo…](https://lawandtechnology.eu/diritto-non-algoritmo-riserva-giurisdizione-umana-ai-act-legge-132-cepej/)
- **Relevance:** Direct CEPEJ-AI. Eight “cepej” strings in the English HTML. The essay opens on the October 2025 European Cyberjustice Network survey figure in the guidelines (**46 per cent** of respondents: generative AI already in use in courts). It restates the four December 2025 statements (exclusive judicial power, access to a human judge, non-binding outputs, transparency in drafting). It then singles out **paragraph 58**. It builds the guidelines on the **2018 Ethical Charter** (fundamental rights, non-discrimination, quality and security, transparency, impartiality and fairness, and “under user control”) and notes the Framework Convention, which it says the European Union ratified on 15 May 2026 and which it says is not yet in force. Italian Law No 132/2025, Article 15, is read beside those texts. The Annex III high-risk timetable is given as **2 December 2027** under Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 of 8 July 2026.
- **Primary-text check:** The sentence the essay attributes to paragraph 58 is in CEPEJ(2025)18Final as retrieved this run: “Judges shall not be required to specifically justify their departure from proposals or recommendations generated by AI systems.” Paragraph 60 repeats the point as a design safeguard. Paragraph 61 remains the documentation duty that Alfonso Peralta Gutiérrez rejected in the 9/16 September essay. This 23 September essay does not cite Peralta or paragraph 61.
- **Quoted snippet:** “The most significant passage, and the least cited, is however paragraph 58, which establishes that judges shall not be required to specifically justify their departure from proposals or recommendations generated by the system.”
- **Quoted snippet:** “The guidelines build on the European Ethical Charter on the Use of Artificial Intelligence in Judicial Systems and their Environment, adopted by the CEPEJ in December 2018, which had already laid down the five principles of respect for fundamental rights, non-discrimination, quality and security, transparency, impartiality and fairness, and the principle ‘under user control’.”
- **Quoted snippet:** “The AI Act places those systems among high-risk systems, the CEPEJ excludes their binding nature, the Italian law reserves interpretation, application and the assessment of evidence to the judge. […] use in judicial offices already stands at 46 per cent. Between the adoption of the tools and their regulation there are therefore two years without regulatory coverage.”
- **Assessment:** Strongest new CEPEJ-AI commentary this week. It is a close reading of the guidelines, not a name-drop, and it moves public argument from paragraph 61 (Peralta, last cycle) to paragraph 58. No LinkedIn or press pickup of this essay was found inside the window.

#### 2.B Luís Nuno Perdigão — Juristech on “Jev” and the Charter’s definition of predictive justice (24 September 2026)

- **Date:** WordPress `date` / `date_gmt` **2026-09-24T15:11:58**; `modified` **2026-09-24T16:13:40**.
- **Author:** Luís Nuno Perdigão (Yoast author field).
- **Type:** Legal-tech commentary.
- **URL:** [O Jev não é justiça preditiva, mas poderá ser outra coisa](https://www.juristech.pt/jev-justica-preditiva-predictive-coding/)
- **Relevance:** Direct CEPEJ-AI. Four “cepej” strings. The Charter glossary is used as the definition of predictive justice (analysis of large volumes of judicial decisions in order to anticipate the outcome of certain disputes). The Charter’s warning that correlations in past decisions are not the same as a court’s legal reasoning is then applied to a new model (“Jev”) offered for document-relevance scoring. The post also cites Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 and the **2 December 2027** deferral of Annex III high-risk obligations, and it says those Article 26 duties are not yet exigible in September 2026.
- **Quoted snippet:** “A Comissão Europeia para a Eficácia da Justiça (CEPEJ), do Conselho da Europa, descreve a justiça preditiva como a análise de grandes quantidades de decisões judiciais para antecipar o desfecho de certos litígios. É a definição constante do glossário da sua Carta Ética. Na Carta Ética Europeia sobre a Utilização da Inteligência Artificial em Sistemas Judiciais, a CEPEJ alerta ainda para o carácter ambíguo do termo: identificar correlações em decisões anteriores não equivale a reproduzir o raciocínio jurídico de um tribunal.”
- **Quoted snippet:** “Há uma ressalva temporal essencial: o Regulamento (UE) 2026/1744 adiou para 2 de dezembro de 2027 a aplicação das obrigações de risco elevado relativas aos sistemas autónomos do Anexo III.”
- **Assessment:** The Charter is doing classificatory work (this tool is not predictive justice), which is a different use from Sapuppo’s paragraph-level reading of the GenAI guidelines. The January syntheses of CEPEJ(2025)18Final and the first Resource Centre report are still 404; this is a new article, not their return.

## 7. Trends and interpretation

1. **The quiet stretch on unique texts broke, in commentary rather than in a new CEPEJ document.** Two practitioner pieces, one day apart, both cite CEPEJ AI work in the body of the argument. Neither is a Council of Europe publication, a court judgment, or a LinkedIn amplification.
2. **Paragraph 58 and paragraph 61 are now both in public argument, from different authors, without cross-citation.** Last cycle, an AIAB member (Peralta) rejected paragraph 61’s documentation duty and kept the Charter’s system-transparency principle. This week, Sapuppo treats paragraph 58’s freedom not to explain a departure from an AI suggestion as the rule that stops a suggestion becoming the default. Both readings are compatible with the primary text. Together they show the December 2025 guidelines being used as a live dispute about judicial reasons, not only as a citation.
3. **The same author corrected a figure she had helped circulate.** Sapuppo’s 11 November 2020 Salvis Juribus essay cited the Charter and a 94 per cent ECHR-prediction claim. The 23 September 2026 essay says that figure is wrong (Aletras et al., 79 per cent; 94 per cent belongs to a commercial NDA test). That is a useful trace of how Charter-adjacent “predictive justice” numbers move through Italian legal media.
4. **Juristech’s CEPEJ relationship is split.** The long syntheses are still gone (fourth week of HTTP 404). A new post uses the Charter glossary with care and states the December 2027 Annex III date correctly. The rest of the week’s AI output on the same site does not mention CEPEJ, including a Portugal case-law-and-AI piece. Absence of the label is no longer uniform on that site; it is selective.
5. **Institutional follow-up to the 7 September Bureau meeting is still one paragraph on the ECHR site.** The UIHJ’s 11 September GT-EVAL note, missed earlier, confirms that CEPEJ’s president briefed an evaluation working group and that GT-CYBERJUST is in the loop for an ICT thematic report. It does not name the Charter or the guidelines. The CEPEJ website item itself could not be read (HTTP 403).
6. **Deadline hygiene in the new posts is better than in the May commentary that is still online.** Both Sapuppo and Perdigão cite Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 and 2 December 2027. People of Internet (17 May 2026) still tells readers that Annex III justice duties arrived on 2 August 2026 and that the AI Office has been issuing joint implementation guidance with CEPEJ. That second claim remains unverified against CEPEJ primary documents.
7. **HTTP `Last-Modified` is still not a publication date.** It moved this week on GamingTechLaw, the Oxford UK atlas, LGSEE, the Peralta APM page, NicFab, and the Juristech homepage. Content dates moved only where WordPress `date` said so (23 and 24 September).
8. **Watchpoints for the next cycles.** WG-CYBERJUST **22–23 October 2026**; 6th European Cyberjustice Network meeting **2 December 2026**; 47th CEPEJ plenary **3–4 December 2026** (evaluation report, per the UIHJ note). Near-term monitoring should watch for (a) a readable CEPEJ-side account of the 7 September Bureau meeting that names the Charter or the guidelines, (b) any reply to Peralta on paragraph 61 or pickup of Sapuppo on paragraph 58, (c) whether Juristech restores the 404 syntheses, (d) English-language pickup of CEPEJ(2026)7, CEPEJ(2026)8, and the Ukrainian Legal Positions Database assistant, and (e) correction of the People of Internet / Megatek August 2026 Annex III claims.

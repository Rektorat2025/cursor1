## 7. Trends and interpretation

1. **The public conversation this week is academic citation of the 2018 Charter, not a new CEPEJ text.** Six articles with journal dates from 2 to 4 November name the Charter. The secretariat did not publish a newsroom item, and the UIHJ did not publish a November note. The December plenary and the 2 December Cyberjustice Network meeting are still ahead of the texts that would change this picture.
2. **The Charter is being used as a short list of principles.** Souza, Silvério and Lima state the five principles in one sentence. Macêdo and Oliveira tie the 2018 text to CNJ Resolution 332/2020 and to the idea that AI stays a tool in the general interest. Velez Juela and Alcivar Cruz call it the first European ethical text specific to judicial systems. Yadav and Anand call it older and more lenient than the EU AI Act, and their five “tenets” do not use the Charter’s own labels for impartiality or user control.
3. **Two citations are bibliographic and one of them has the year wrong.** Kaplan enters the Charter twice, in 2019 and in 2018, and does not discuss it. Aguirre Espinosa and Agudelo Mejía date it 2021 and point at the CEPEJ Charter URL. A count of “mentions” that stopped at the reference list would treat these as equal to the four discussions. They are weaker references.

## 7. Trends and interpretation

1. **Second consecutive quiet week for dated CEPEJ-AI mentions.** After mid-July’s EEEI and Rule of Law signals, the 20–27 July and 27 July–3 August windows produced no new date-verified direct references. That fits a midsummer lull more than a decline in the underlying relevance of CEPEJ instruments.
2. **AI Act hard law moved; CEPEJ soft law was not the citation vehicle.** Enforcement and Article 50 transparency began this week, while Annex III justice-related high-risk duties were pushed to December 2027. Public commentary focused on Commission/AI Office enforcement and Omnibus deferrals, not on the CEPEJ Charter or generative-AI guidelines. The deferred justice high-risk timeline may postpone the next wave of secondary CEPEJ citations that often accompany hard-law compliance writing.
3. **Date hygiene remains essential.** HTTP `Last-Modified` again moved on LGSEE (3 Aug), Juristech (31 Jul), Uniandes (28 Jul), and Oxford Spain (2 Aug) without matching content `dateModified`/`datePublished` changes—so headers alone are insufficient to count a weekly mention. Search also continued to resurface standing CEPEJ PDFs and older LinkedIn posts as if current.
4. **Human-centred framing is stable in carry-over sources.** EEEI, Kunda, Nastić, and Oxford Spain material continue to treat CEPEJ instruments as the operational reference for non-substitution, oversight, transparency, and data sovereignty—especially when national rules are mapped onto the Charter.
5. **Watchpoints for the next cycles.** Per EEEI’s WG-CYBERJUST reporting: next Cyberjust meeting **22–23 October 2026**; 6th European Cyberjustice Network meeting **2 December 2026**. Near-term monitoring should also watch for justice-sector guidance that bridges CEPEJ tools and AI Act Article 50 / deferred Annex III duties.

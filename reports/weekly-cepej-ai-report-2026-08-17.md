- **Date:** Issue imprint: “III trimestre 2026 — chiuso in redazione in luglio 2026.” Article PDFs `Last-Modified` **29 July 2026** (Schmidt, Radu, and full issue PDF). Outside this window.
- **Editor / publisher:** Maria Giuliana Civinini (issue editor); *Questione Giustizia* / Magistratura democratica (ISSN 1972-5531)
- **Type:** Italian judicial quarterly; English translations available for several articles
- **Issue PDF:** [La giustizia alla sfida delle nuove tecnologie dell’informazione](https://www.questionegiustizia.it/data/rivista/pdf/61/3_2026_qg_la-giustizia-alla-sfida-delle-nuove-tecnologie-dell-informazione.pdf)
- **Direct CEPEJ-AI contributions (non-exhaustive):**
  - **Daniel Schmidt** (Secretary of CEPEJ-GT-CYBERJUST), “Does soft law matter? The Guidelines of the Council of Europe’s European Commission for the Efficiency of Justice (CEPEJ)” — [English PDF](https://www.questionegiustizia.it/data/rivista/articoli/1395/3_2026_qg_schmidt-eng.pdf). Lists the 2018 Ethical Charter, the 2023 Assessment Tool, the 2024 generative-AI information note, and the December 2025 generative-AI court guidelines.   - **Anca Radu** (CEPEJ independent scientific expert), “Best practices in promoting the use of AI applications in the field of justice” — [English PDF](https://www.questionegiustizia.it/data/rivista/articoli/1397/3_2026_qg_radu-eng.pdf).   - **Alfonso Peralta Gutiérrez** (CEPEJ AIAB member), “Come la tecnologia può migliorare la qualità della giustizia alla luce dell’esperienza spagnola” — [Italian PDF](https://www.questionegiustizia.it/data/rivista/articoli/1415/3_2026_qg_gutierrez.pdf). Quotes the Charter on responsible use and cites the Resource Centre ranking of Spanish tools.
  - **Domenico Cardamone**, contribution in the same issue — [PDF](https://www.questionegiustizia.it/data/rivista/articoli/1391/3_2026_qg_cardamone.pdf). Situates the 2018 Charter and the 2024 generative-AI information note ahead of the Framework Convention.
  - **Giuseppe Longo**, contribution in the same issue — [PDF](https://www.questionegiustizia.it/data/rivista/articoli/1421/3_2026_qg_longo.pdf). Footnotes the 2018 Charter as the CEPEJ reference for human-centred judicial AI, alongside Italy’s law 132/2025, art. 15.
- **Quoted snippet (Schmidt):** “One particularly interesting case is the Ethical AI Charter, which was mentioned above and addressed several crucial issues prior to their codification into international standards such as the Council of Europe’s Framework Convention on AI and the EU’s AI Act.”
- **Assessment:** Strongest recent **judicial-profession** cluster citing CEPEJ AI instruments. Date is late July 2026, so it is **not** an in-window hit; treat as catch-up documentation.

#### 2.B CEPEJ 2nd AIAB report — CEPEJ(2026)7 (adopted 18–19 June 2026)

- **Date:** Adopted at the 46th CEPEJ plenary, Šibenik, **18–19 June 2026**. Cover: “© Council of Europe, June 2026.” Public PDF now retrievable; HTTP `Last-Modified` could not be read (Cloudflare **403** on direct curl). First appearance of this URL in this monitoring series.
- **Author/publisher:** CEPEJ Artificial Intelligence Advisory Board (AIAB); CEPEJ
- **Type:** Official CEPEJ monitoring report
- **URL:** [2nd Report on the Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in the Judiciary, CEPEJ(2026)7](https://rm.coe.int/cepej-2026-7-en-second-aiab-report-2026/48802cbf1a)
- **Relevance:** Follow-up to the first Resource Centre report. Positions the AIAB as “a key element of the roadmap to ensure an appropriate follow-up of the ‘European Ethical Charter …’ (referred to as ‘CEPEJ AI Charter’)”. Records 176 listed tools (51 added in 2025), growing LLM use for legal research and drafting, weak impact-evaluation capacity in states, and “Shadow AI” as defined in the 2025 generative-AI guidelines. Aligns GPAI/LLM provider duties with those guidelines and notes Annex III high-risk justice duties postponed to December 2027.
- **Quoted snippet:** “These provisions are in line with the CEPEJ Guidelines on the use of generative artificial intelligence for the courts, which calls for the transparent, verified, and cautious use of LLMs in the judicial context.”
- **Assessment:** Primary-source CEPEJ AI output. Adoption date is June 2026 (outside window). Public availability of the final PDF is newly confirmed this run; the exact first-publication timestamp on `rm.coe.int` could not be independently verified because of Cloudflare blocking.

## 7. Trends and interpretation

1. **Fourth consecutive quiet week for dated CEPEJ-AI mentions inside the monitoring window.** After mid-July’s EEEI and Rule of Law signals, the 20 July–17 August stretch produced no new date-verified direct references *published in-week*. That remains consistent with a midsummer lull in news cycles rather than a decline in the underlying relevance of CEPEJ instruments.
2. **Catch-up, not a spike: Italian judicial scholarship and the second AIAB report.** The important development this run is documentary, not chronological. *Questione Giustizia* 3/2026 (closed July) is a CEPEJ-insider and practitioner symposium: Schmidt (working-group secretary) argues that Charter soft law shaped later hard law; Radu maps the Charter → Assessment Tool → AIAB → Resource Centre pipeline; Gutiérrez and others treat the Charter as the ethical baseline for national digital-justice programmes.

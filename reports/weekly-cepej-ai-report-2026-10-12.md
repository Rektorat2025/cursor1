#### 2.A Andrés Gabriel Varas Quijón — the Santiago prompt and the 2018 Charter (7–8 October 2026)

- **Date:** Zenodo `created` **2026-10-08T12:06:52Z**. Record `publication_date` **2026-10-07**. Version DOI and concept DOI share the created timestamp. The abstract says the judgment became public on 7 October 2026.
- **Author:** Andrés Gabriel Varas Quijón, Doctor en Derecho (Universidad Nacional de Mar del Plata); Máster en Derecho, Universitat de València and Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona. ORCID 0009-0001-5248-8736.
- **Type:** Preprint, 12 pages, Spanish with an English abstract. Not a Council of Europe document.
- **URLs:** [Zenodo 23223373](https://zenodo.org/records/23223373) · [DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23223373](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23223373) (concept DOI [10.5281/zenodo.23223372](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23223372))
- **Relevance:** Direct CEPEJ-AI. The PDF contains **14** “CEPEJ” strings. The incident it analyses is a resolution of the Novena Sala of the Santiago Court of Appeals, signed 6 October 2026 in a civil appeal, whose first pages contained a generative-AI prompt for an *amparo* judgment (“Eres un Juez de la Corte de Apelaciones que debe redactar…”), a 750-word cap, and alternative arguments depending on the outcome. The chamber requested a report. The paper says it does not judge the persons involved.
- **What it cites, checked against the PDF:** the 2018 Ethical Charter and its five principles, with the user-control principle glossed as requiring the judge to be an informed actor and owner of the decision. It also cites Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Annex III, point 8(a), and recital 61 (the final decision must remain a human activity); the England and Wales judicial AI guidance (2023, updated 2025); Colombian Constitutional Court T-323/2024; Brazilian National Council of Justice Resolutions 332 (2020) and 615 (2025); and UNESCO’s 2025 court-AI guidance. Footnote 10 mentions Ricardo Lillo’s April 2026 Judicial Academy material as a synthesis that includes CEPEJ. The bibliography lists the 2018 Charter and does not list CEPEJ(2025)18Final or CEPEJ(2026)7. Zero hits for Scarpa, Peralta, Sapuppo, Bernaziuk, “18Final”, or “176”.
- **Quoted snippet:** “En diciembre de 2018 la Comisión Europea para la Eficacia de la Justicia adoptó la Carta Ética sobre el uso de la inteligencia artificial en los sistemas judiciales, con cinco principios: respeto de los derechos fundamentales, no discriminación, calidad y seguridad, transparencia, y «bajo control del usuario» — el principio que exige que el juez sea un actor informado y dueño de su decisión, nunca un ejecutor de la sugerencia de la máquina.”
- **Quoted snippet:** “«Eres un juez» es la delegación del rol que la CEPEJ excluye; la instrucción de argumentar en el sentido previamente decidido invierte la supervisión humana que el Reglamento exige; y la ausencia de toda mención al uso de la herramienta en la sentencia contradice la transparencia que todas las reglas europeas comparten.”
- **Quoted snippet (decalogue, rule 5):** “Revelar el uso. La transparencia es la condición del derecho de contradicción (T-323/2024; CEPEJ): la parte tiene derecho a saber cómo se construyó la decisión que la afecta.”
- **Assessment:** The week’s fullest Charter reading. The “never an executor of the machine’s suggestion” sentence and the disclosure rule are the author’s application. The Charter’s user-control principle, as published by CEPEJ, precludes a prescriptive approach and requires users to be informed actors in control of their choices. Varas does not quote CEPEJ(2025)18Final paragraph 58 (no duty to justify departure) or paragraph 61 (documentation and the parties’ right to know). His recital 61 is the EU AI Act, not the guidelines.

## 7. Trends and interpretation

1. **The week’s CEPEJ-AI references are commentary on a court incident, plus one procedural article.** A prompt left in a Santiago Court of Appeals resolution on 6 October produced a news wave on 7 October and, on 7–8 October, two legal texts that reach for the 2018 Charter. The news pages that were opened do not name CEPEJ. The Charter appears once the writers move from the fact of the prompt to a comparative rule.
2. **User control is doing the work that paragraph 58 did in September.** Varas and Scarpa both stop at the 2018 principle. Varas uses it to say a prompt that says “you are a judge” delegates a role the Charter excludes, and he attaches a disclosure rule to CEPEJ together with T-323/2024. Scarpa uses it to say professionals must be able to review the data and not be bound by the output, and that Chilean article 17 still lacks operational duties. Neither cites the December 2025 guidelines. Neither cites Sapuppo’s reading of the departure sentence.
3. **CEPEJ paragraph 61 and AI Act recital 61 are easy to confuse, and this week’s preprint uses the recital.** Varas’s “considerando 61” is the EU AI Act statement that the final decision must remain human. It is not CEPEJ(2025)18Final paragraph 61. Stepanova’s call for notice to the parties is the closest this week comes to that paragraph, and the article does not number it.
4. **The December 2025 guidelines have a second public citation, still without a paragraph debate.** Stepanova cites CEPEJ(2025)18Final and proposes notice, registration, verification, responsibility, and a right to object. Bernaziuk, on 2 October, treated paragraph 61 as a positive documentation duty. Peralta, in September, rejected that duty. These texts still do not cite one another.
5. **CEPEJ(2026)7 did not gain another public reading.** The 176-tool count and the “no fully automated decision-making” sentence remain, in this series, Bernaziuk’s 2 October citation. The mediation deposit of 5 October is a CEPEJ citation and is not an AI citation.
6. **A site that covers AI discipline for lawyers can still omit CEPEJ.** Juristech’s 6 October post on false citations and disciplinary responsibility does not mention CEPEJ. Thirteen in-window posts contain zero “cepej”. The January syntheses remain HTTP 404 for a sixth week. The only live Juristech CEPEJ article is still the 24 September Jev post.
7. **The stale Annex III claim is unchanged.** People of Internet (17 May 2026, last HTTP touch 20 August) still carries the claim that Annex III justice duties arrived in August 2026. Megatek returned HTTP 429 again, so that page was not re-read. CEPEJ(2026)7, as checked in prior weeks, places the main high-risk duties in December 2027.
8. **HTTP `Last-Modified` moved again without a new publication date** on LGSEE, NicFab, GamingTechLaw, the Oxford UK Atlas page, and the Peralta APM page. Content dates moved only where Zenodo `created` or a page byline said so (5 October 11:05 UTC, 7–8 October).

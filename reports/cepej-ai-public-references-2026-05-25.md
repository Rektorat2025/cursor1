## 7. Trends and interpretation

1. From ethics to implementation: Mentions increasingly frame CEPEJ's AI work as a practical governance stack: Ethical Charter -> Assessment Tool -> Resource Centre/AIAB report -> generative AI guidelines -> planned Observatory on AI in Justice.
2. Capacity-building is becoming central: The EuroDIG session and EEEI roadmap both point to training curricula, HELP resources, UNESCO/EJTN cooperation, and practical competence for judges and lawyers.
3. Generative AI has moved from hypothetical to operational risk: Public judicial AI stories this week focused on hallucinated citations, prompt injection, deepfakes, transparency, and verification. These issues directly reinforce CEPEJ's emphasis on human oversight, non-binding AI outputs, traceability, and secure/public-control infrastructure.
4. Observatory model is gaining salience: CEPEJ's Resource Centre is repeatedly referenced as a public evidence base and is now being discussed as the foundation for an Observatory on AI in Justice.
5. International convergence: UNESCO, OECD, EU/EUR-Lex, BIICL, Biodiritto, and Universidad de los Andes references show alignment around core safeguards: human control, transparency, accountability, non-discrimination, risk assessment, and judicial independence.
6. Social visibility gap: Public LinkedIn-accessible results show substantial AI-in-courts discussion, but current direct references to CEPEJ are sparse. CEPEJ may need more direct social amplification around its 2025 generative AI guidelines and 2026-2027 WG-CYBERJUST roadmap.

## 7. Trends and interpretation

- **From principles to implementation.** Public references continue to start with the 2018 CEPEJ Ethical Charter, but the discussion has moved toward concrete implementation instruments: assessment questions, generative AI guidance, and judicial training.
- **Generative AI is the dominant AI subtopic.** LinkedIn, CEPEJ tools, EEEI, UIHJ, EuroDIG, and third-party summaries all focus on generative AI in courts rather than earlier predictive-justice framing.
- **Human oversight and judicial independence are repeated safeguards.** The same vocabulary recurs across sources: effective human oversight, legal certainty, transparency, traceability, non-discrimination, data protection, and non-binding AI outputs.
- **Data sovereignty and public control are becoming more prominent.** The EEEI account of the 2026-2027 roadmap and the CEPEJ generative AI guidelines both point away from unmanaged general-purpose tools and toward jurisdiction-specific, public-controlled infrastructure.
- **CEPEJ's AI work is increasingly linked to broader European AI governance.** Sources connect CEPEJ outputs to the Council of Europe AI Framework Convention, EU AI Act high-risk systems, HUDERIA, and fundamental-rights impact assessment.
- **Current-week media signal is low.** Searches did not show a surge of new press coverage during 2026-06-01 to 2026-06-08. The public conversation appears to be steady institutional uptake rather than a new news cycle.
